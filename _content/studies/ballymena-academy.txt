# Overview

Ballymena Academy appointed DHD to clarify, modernise, and expand their visual identity, while honouring their 200-year history.

As the Brand Designer, I worked with the client to separate fact from fiction, uncover the truly valuable components of their existing identity, and introduce a new brand mark and guidelines to help protect the brand going forward. As multiple creatives assist in the rollout of the new identity, I continue to oversee and direct the process.

# Challenge

The school’s traditional coat of arms, and the visual elements derived from it, held deep historical value, but lacked flexibility across digital and print applications. Changes to the brand required consultation with over 90 members of staff and the board of governors.

# Process

Questionnaires allowed me to consult with the largest stakeholder group I had ever worked with.

On-site visits revealed almost 20 variations of the school coat of arms, along with an overused primary colour palette.

Working with the client, we sourced the original coat of arms and redrew it, while making improvements and documenting its meaning.

After revisiting and testing many of the school’s previous brand marks, we settled on the shield mark.

# Solution

The shield mark felt refreshing and ready for the online world, yet was distilled from the existing coat of arms.

Variable fonts allowed us to tweak the brand expression from serif to sans serif, depending on the audience.

A colourful secondary colour palette complemented the navy, red, and white synonymous with the brand, while giving flexibility and expression.

I repurposed the mantling from the coat of arms and used it to frame content, creating a unique and instantly recognisable compositional element for brand touchpoints.
